## Overview

- 3 hours' drive with no traffic
- East side is relatively high (more time at 8–9k ft) and more crowded
- West side is lower (lots in the 7–8k ft range), quieter, and trails can be rougher
- Decent options for different trails; covering most of the park is a 48-mile loop
- Bear can required
- June–September is the best time to visit; July to early September is peak

[Overview article](https://exploringwild.com/backpacking-desolation-wilderness/)

## Areas and routes

The PCT runs through the east and the Rubicon Trail through the west. They join at Lake Aloha in the south and can be linked in the north with the Velma Trail for a larger loop.

- **PCT (east)**: stays relatively high, excellent views going lake to lake, better-maintained trails, more crowded (never far from other people).
- **Rubicon Trail (west)**: stays low in the Rubicon River drainage. Less scenic, but gives access (via a steep climb) to smaller, less crowded lakes. Dirt road (2WD should be fine) to Wrights Lake, where you can hike over Rockbound Pass.

### Southeast

Echo Lakes, Ralston Peak or Glen Alpine trailheads (3 hr drive). Lake Aloha is the star attraction.

### Northeast

Bayview, Eagle Falls and Meeks trailheads (3 hr drive). The popular Velma and Fontanillis Lake area. Bayview is the smoother, more gradual of the two. Middle and lower Velma zones are beautiful but crowded.

- [Genevieve and Stony Ridge Lakes, Phipps Peak, PCT loop](https://www.alltrails.com/explore/custom-routes/lake-genevieve-stony-ridge-phipps-pct-loop-from-meeks-bay-f18a6d1): 22 mi, 3,500 ft gain, 6,200–8,800 ft

### West

Wrights Lake / Twin Lakes trailhead. A dirt road, but accessible (when seasonally open) to standard 2WD cars. Much less crowded than the east, with some pretty rough trails in places.

- [Rockbound Pass, Lake Schmidell, Horseshoe Lake loop](https://www.alltrails.com/explore/custom-routes/rockbound-pass-lake-schmidell-horseshoe-lake-loop-4b86ca4): 24 mi, 4,800 ft gain, 6,900–8,500 ft. Could base camp at Lake Schmidell and day hike the loop around Horseshoe Lake, or carry gear and add an out-and-back to secluded Highland Lake at the north end.

### From any trailhead

- [Lake Aloha, Rubicon Trail, PCT, Dicks Pass central loop](https://www.alltrails.com/explore/trail/us/california/bayview-dicks-peak-jacks-peak-loop): 27 mi, 5,200 ft gain, 6,900–9,300 ft
- [Big double loop](https://www.alltrails.com/explore/custom-routes/desolation-wilderness-double-loop-852c296): 48 mi, 9k ft gain, 6,500–9,300 ft. Glen Alpine is the most direct start. From a different trailhead, consider skipping the southernmost section and taking the PCT from Lake Aloha to Gilmore Lake.

Wrights Lake area construction in 2026 may temporarily close the Rockbound and Twin Lakes trailheads.

## Permits

- Required year-round.
- Quota from the Friday before Memorial Day to Sep 30: 70% on Recreation.gov up to 6 months ahead, 30% walk-in.
- $5/person (1 night) or $10/person (2–14 nights) + $6 reservation fee. Book early; popular zones sell out on summer weekends.
- Off-season: permit still required, no quota.
- Camping is dispersed, but your first night must be in your permitted zone (45 zones).

## When to go

Mid-July to early October; snowed in roughly Nov–Jun.

- **June**: feasible in low-snow years. Keep an eye on snow levels and be prepared to turn back. Expect wet trails, more mosquitoes, harder water crossings and snowfields. The east side is safer since it's more popular and well trodden.
- **July**: usually safe in typical snow years; may still be early for the highest spots in high-snow years. Mosquitoes still bad. Wildflowers going.
- **August**: warm, often mild weather. Snow has usually melted from the highest points; mosquitoes less intense. Increasing risk of wildfire smoke later in summer.
- **September**: nights can be quite chilly. Weather is often clear and stable early in the month. Late September brings a risk of early winter storms.

## Other

- **Fires**: campfires prohibited; stoves only (the wilderness permit covers stove use).
- **Cell service**: spotty; often a signal on ridges, none in basins.
- **Hazards**: thunderstorms, early snow, exposed granite, bold bears at trailheads, mosquitoes in early summer.
