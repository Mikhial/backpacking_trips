## Overview

- The lesser-known cousin of Yosemite Valley
- Follows the Tuolumne River the whole way, from calm emerald pools to whitewater and thundering falls, under towering canyon walls
- Best done in 3–4 days
- Great swimming holes, but parts of the river are dangerous. Rattlesnakes in Pate Valley.[^reddit]

## Getting there

- **Drive**: 3 hr 40 min to White Wolf or 4 hr 15 min to Tuolumne Meadows

### Getting to the trailhead[^bft-trailhead][^reddit][^alltrails]

Point to point, so sort out transport before applying for the permit.

- **Two cars**: White Wolf is 25 miles west of Tuolumne Meadows on Hwy 120.
- **Bus**: leave a car at Tuolumne Meadows and take a bus to White Wolf.
  - **[YARTS](https://www.yarts.com/bus_routes/highway-395-120e/)**: $12 · Tuolumne Meadows Visitor Center · request pickup/drop off at White Wolf
  - **[Meadows Hikers Bus](https://www.travelyosemite.com/activities/year-round/guided-bus-tours#meadows-hikers-bus)**: ~$20 · Tuolumne Meadows Store
  - Finishing at White Wolf can mean waiting until afternoon for a bus.

- **Hitchhiking**: legal in Yosemite. Easier finishing at Tuolumne Meadows (near the store) than at White Wolf, where you walk up to the highway and about half a mile east to find a safe pullout.
- **Parking**: the Lembert Dome lot is no overnight parking; park on the side road next to it (only 3–4 bear boxes).

**Which direction**[^bft-direction][^alltrails][^reddit]

- **Tuolumne Meadows to White Wolf**: 
  - Mostly a mellow downhill with the best scenery in the first half
  - Whole climb out of Pate Valley on the last day, when your pack is lightest.
  - Steep switchbacks, hot and exposed, with no water after Morrison Creek.
  - Harder to get back to your car.
  - Permit name: Glen Aulin -> Cold Canyon/Waterwheel if you’re camping past Glen Aulin High Sierra Camp the first night or Glen Aulin if you’re camping at Glen Aulin (5.3 miles).

- **White Wolf to Tuolumne Meadows**
  - The more popular direction
  - A steep 3,500 ft drop to Pate Valley on day one with a full pack, then uphill most of the way, with water and the falls ahead of you. 
  - Scenery and swimming holes get better as you go, but the uphill finish is hard in the heat. 
  - Easier to get back to your car.
  - Permit name: White Wolf -> Pate Valley if you’re camping past Harden Lake the first night, or White Wolf -> Smith Valley if you’re camping at Harden Lake (2.8 miles).

## Camping[^reddit]

- A lot of people camp illegally in this canyon. Legal sites are there, you just need to look; they're often easier to spot from above on the switchbacks coming down. Large flat areas near the river probably have sites. Remember: 100 ft.
- Some camps get re-established where camping isn't legal and people assume they're fine, like the ones along the river just down from Glen Aulin (inside the no-camping zone).
- At the trail camps, use the established sites instead of setting up in unimpacted spots next to them.
- Fire restrictions below 6,000 ft: no campfires in Pate Valley or below a little west of Return Creek.

## How this connects to Tuolumne Meadows[^sierrarec]

Glen Aulin puts you back within reach of the Tuolumne Meadows trails and high country, like Cathedral Lakes and Lyell Canyon.

## Permits

Required year-round. Pick up in person. Easier to get in September (fewer crowds).[^bft-permits]

- **Weekly lottery** (60%): 24 weeks (168 days) ahead, on Recreation.gov.
- **Reservations** (40%): released 7 days ahead on Recreation.gov.
- **Applying**: give your starting trailhead and where you'll camp the first night.[^bft-permits]
  - **From Tuolumne Meadows**: the Glen Aulin backpackers' camp, or pass through and camp beyond it (private riverside sites not far past). One reviewer's permit required Glen Aulin.[^alltrails]
  - **From White Wolf**: aim for Pate Valley, to spread the climbing over the remaining days.

**Cost**: ~$10 application + $5/person.
