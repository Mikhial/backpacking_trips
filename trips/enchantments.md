## Overview

- Point to point, west to east: Stuart Lake Trailhead, up Aasgard Pass, through the Core Zone, down past Snow Lakes to Snow Lakes Trailhead.
- East to west is also possible, but the shuttle is harder to schedule, it adds an extra 2k feet of climbing, and going down Aasgard Pass is difficult.
- Day hike or overnight. A day hike is incredibly challenging but needs only a free permit; overnights let you go at your own pace but need the lottery.
- Tougher than Mt Whitney, but without the altitude.

## Getting there

- **Fly**: to Seattle (2 hr), then drive 2.5 hr to Leavenworth. Flying to Wenatchee or taking Amtrak from Seattle are other options, but don't seem worth the hassle.
- **Stay**: in Leavenworth, which has two small sporting goods stores, with dispersed camping nearby. Snow Lakes Trailhead is about 5 mi (15 min) up Icicle Road; Stuart Lake Trailhead about 13 mi (40 min).

### Shuttle

- [Leavenworth Shuttle](https://www.leavenworthshuttle.com/mountain) runs between the two trailheads in about 20 minutes. Park at your finishing trailhead and shuttle to the start.
- Book at least 24 hours ahead, once you have a permit and lodging.
- Runs at 5:00, 5:45, 7:30 and 8:15am (plus 6:45am on weekends). For the reverse direction or other times, call to schedule. No service after 7pm.
- $60 alone, $30/person otherwise, plus tip.
- Snow Lakes Trailhead lot holds about 50 cars. Arrive by 4:30am for the 5am shuttle for a good chance at a spot; overflow parking is on the street.
- **Parking**: a Northwest Forest Pass or an America the Beautiful pass. Overnight permits include two parking passes. [How to pay](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/okanogan-wenatchee/newsroom/stories/digital-payment-now-required-most-recreation-sites).

## When to go[^hikingguy]

Skip the day of if rain is solidly forecast; wet granite slabs are unpleasant to descend. Thunderstorms have no real shelter in the Core Zone.

## Permits

- **Day hike**: free, self-issue at the trailhead, no limit.
- **Overnight Lottery**:
  - Permits required May 15 – Oct 31.
  - Some of the hardest to get in the US: 1–12% lottery odds.
  - Apply Feb 15 – Mar 1 on [Recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/233273) with your dates.
  - Winners are notified Mar 17 and must claim by Mar 31.
- **Unclaimed**: released Apr 1 around 7am PT.
- **Cancellations**: after May 15. [Outdoor Status](https://outdoorstatus.com/alerts/us/washington/alpine-lakes-wilderness/enchantment-permit/) texts you about them, or check Recreation.gov.
- **Daily lottery**: apply the [day before](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/445863) on your phone, from inside the geofence (within a mile of the Leavenworth Ranger Station).

**Cost**: $6 lottery application + $5/person/day overnight. Day hikes are free but need a $5 Northwest Forest Pass to park.

**Zones**: Five zones. A Core permit lets you camp in any zone; the others only in their own. Core is by far the best and aim to spend the most time there.

**Odds, best to worst**:
  - **Camps**: Stuart, Snow, Colchuck, Core
  - **Days**: Sun, Mon, Tue, Wed, Sat, Thu, Fri
  - **Months**: May, Oct, Jun, Sep, Jul, Aug

## Trips by permit

Day hiking is allowed in every zone, so each permit can still reach the Core by day, and any permit can start and finish at either trailhead. Camping is dispersed and there is usually a toilet nearby.

- **Core**: camp anywhere.
  - **1 night**: up Aasgard to a Core camp (6.5 mi), then across the Core and down (12.5 mi).
  - **2-4 nights**: Colchuck, then 1–2 nights in the Core (a free day for Prusik Pass or Little Annapurna), then Snow Lakes.
- **Snow**: the best one if no Core permit.
  - **1 night**: up Aasgard and through the Core to a Snow Lakes (11.5 mi) or Nada Lake camp (12.5 mi), then 6.5-7.5 mi out.
  - **1 night, east to west**: Camp at Snow Lakes camp (6.5–8 mi), then up through the Core and down Aasgard (10.5-12 mi). Camping at the Core end of Upper Snow Lake, near the bridge, evens the days out. Need to call shuttle to schedule this direction.
  - **2 nights**: in from Snow Lakes Trailhead (6–7 mi, 3,600 ft up), a day up the slabs to Lake Viviane and the Lower Core and back, then out. No Aasgard and no shuttle.
- **Colchuck**:
  - **1 night**: 5 mi to Colchuck Lake, then the rest of the traverse (14 mi) with Aasgard first thing.
  - **2 nights**: a day up Aasgard into the Upper Core and back (descends Aasgard), then out the way you came.
- **Stuart**: the easiest to get.
  - **1 night**: camp along Mountaineer Creek (1–2 mi in), then ~17 mi out over the traverse. A night out, but the big day is barely shorter.
  - **1 night, east to west**: 17 miles to Mountaineer Creek. Will be very hard doing almost entire traverse in opposite direction.
- **No overnight permit**: the traverse as a day hike, or try the daily lottery from Leavenworth over a few days.
