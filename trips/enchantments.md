## Overview

- Point to point, west to east: Stuart Lake Trailhead, up Aasgard Pass, through the Core Zone, down past Snow Lakes to Snow Lakes Trailhead. This way has about 2,000 ft less climbing.
- Day hike or overnight. A day hike is incredibly challenging but needs only a free permit; overnights let you go at your own pace but need the lottery.
- Tougher than Mt Whitney, but without the altitude.
- *Also west to east: the loose-dirt downhill is worse than going up Aasgard, the shuttle is easier this way, and it works as an all-day hike.*
- *Day hikers carry little and refill water along the way, but only get a taste. Overnights mean more gear to carry uphill, but are the best way to appreciate it.*
- *Crowds: hundreds of day hikers some days, weekends the worst. On a mid-September Tuesday the guide's author counted 35 other people on the whole route.*
- *Worth doing at least once, especially with an overnight permit.*
- *Overnight itineraries: 2 days, Core; 3 days, Colchuck → Core; 4 days, Colchuck → 2 nights Core; 5 days, Colchuck → 2 nights Core → Snow; 6+, more time in Core.*
- *Favorite Core Zone camps: around Inspiration and Perfection Lakes.*
- *Planning: work out how much time you have and apply for the lottery with your ideal dates (the author aims for 2–5 days in late Aug or early Sep). If you don't get one, train for the day hike and try again next year.*

## When to go

First time, wait until August.

*Snow can last all season. In September 2026 a hiker [died in extreme conditions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yDVTYoNFIto), and there have been several deaths in icy shoulder seasons. Even with winter hiking experience, don't show up without local knowledge.*

## Permits

Overnight permits are required May 15 – Oct 31, and are some of the hardest to get in the US: 1–12% lottery odds. Day hikes need only a free, self-issued permit.

- **Day hike**: free, self-issue at the trailhead, no limit. Put one copy in the box and keep the stub on your pack. *Pencils are usually in the box.*
- **Lottery**: apply Feb 15 – Mar 1 on [Recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/233273) with your dates. Winners are notified Mar 17 and must claim by Mar 31. *May open at 7am PT (unconfirmed).*
- **Unclaimed**: released Apr 1 around 7am PT; be at your computer when it opens.
- **Cancellations**: after May 15. [Outdoor Status](https://outdoorstatus.com/alerts/us/washington/alpine-lakes-wilderness/enchantment-permit/) texts you about them, or check Recreation.gov.
- **Daily lottery**: apply the [day before](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/445863) on your phone, from inside the geofence (within a mile of the Leavenworth Ranger Station, per the trail guide).

**Cost**: $6 lottery application (non-refundable, even if you don't win) + $5/person/day overnight. Day hikes are free but need a Northwest Forest Pass to park.

**Camping**: zones (Core, Colchuck, Snow, Stuart, Eightmile/Caroline), with dispersed sites and usually a toilet nearby. A Core permit lets you camp in any zone; the others only in their own. One permit per season. *Groups of 8 at most.*

*Quota per day: Core 24 people (16 through the lottery, 8 through the daily lottery); Colchuck 3 groups, Stuart 4, Snow 5, Eightmile/Caroline 3. One permit in each zone is held for the daily lottery.*

*Cancel 22+ days ahead for a refund (not the $6); no refund within 21 days. The daily lottery page says no refunds at all. The zone and entry date can't be changed after booking.*

Best odds to worst: zones Stuart, Snow, Colchuck, Core; days Sun, Mon, Tue, Wed, Sat, Thu, Fri; months May, Oct, Jun, Sep, Jul, Aug. *The guide's author has won an overnight permit 3 times in 15 years.*

[Permit page](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/233273)

## Route

- **Stuart Lake Trailhead to Colchuck Lake** (5 mi): gradual climb along Mountaineer Creek under trees.
- **Aasgard Pass** (1 mi): extremely steep 2,000 ft climb on loose dirt. A north-facing gully that can hold snow and ice.
- **Core Zone** (3.5 mi): fairly flat, through alpine lakes and jagged peaks.
- **Snow Zone** (4 mi): steep descent on granite slabs following cairns, then a cruise past Snow Lakes and Nada Lake.
- **The descent** (5.5 mi): long, mostly well-graded trail down Snow Creek.

*Reported distances range from 18 to over 20 miles; side trips push it toward 20.*

## *Directions*

*Stuart Lake Trailhead to Colchuck Lake:*

- *Stuart Lake Trail #1599 starts at the far end of the lot: rocky, rooty, mostly uphill.*
- *About 1.4 mi: a scree field on the right. Don't take trails into it; the trail continues left.*
- *About 1.7 mi: cross Mountaineer Creek.*
- *About 2.3 mi: junction with Colchuck Lake Trail 1599.1; go left. Shortly after, a bridge into a talus field: make the hard right after the bridge and go around its outside.*
- *About 4 mi: the Colchuck Lake area. Go around the right shore. A short side trail goes to a lake viewpoint. Colchuck means "cold lake" in Chinook Jargon; the local tribe was the Wenatchi.*
- *Past Little Colchuck Lake, side trails go to viewpoints and tent sites; a GPS track helps. At the far end, cross the boulders of the moraine under Colchuck Glacier. The far side weaves through vegetation.*

*Aasgard Pass:*

- *The official name is Colchuck Pass. "Aasgard" came from local climbers Bill and Peggy Stark in the 1950s.*
- *Avoid the stream on the right (if you're crossing a stream, you're too far right), stay left of the larch grove, then go straight up and across to the right at the top. Look for the next cairn and the most trodden path, and check GPS.*
- *Loose dirt: anchor on solid rocks; poles help. Yell "rock!" if you knock one loose. If heights bother you, stop on a flat rock.*
- *When the larch grove is level with you on the right, you're about 40% done. Past it, sheer granite on the left, then false summits before the true top.*
- *At the top, the first flat section, then Isolation Lake comes into view.*

*Core Zone:*

- *Upper Enchantments: Isolation Lake on the right, fed by Snow Creek Glacier (you follow Snow Creek to the end). Tranquil Lake on the left is easier to refill at. Follow cairns downhill past unnamed lakes; snow bridges over the outlets may remain. A toilet with views down to Crystal Lake.*
- *Middle Enchantments: a steep chute down to Inspiration Lake, around its far shore, then around the far shore of Perfection Lake. Prusik Peak is above, named for Karl Prusik of the prusik knot; overnighters can hike under it to [Prusik Pass](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/washington/prusik-pass-trail). Then a short hop to Sprite Lake.*
- *The lakes have been stocked with golden and cutthroat trout for about 100 years. Fishing needs a license.*
- *Lower Enchantments: cairns over granite slabs, emerald-green Leprechaun Lake on the right, the ledges, then Lake Viviane below. A narrow ledge around the nose of the cliff, then a rebar section: hold the rebar if it's wet, walk if it's dry; after the turn you may go down on your butt.*

*Snow Zone:*

- *The Forest Service calls the Core Zone to Upper Snow Lake stretch a primitive cairn-marked route, for experienced hikers only.*
- *Steep granite slabs following cairns, then steep dirt into the trees. Snow Creek stays on your right until Snow Lakes.*
- *Flat trail along Snow Lakes. At the far end of Upper Snow Lake, cross the masonry dam, then a short gradual climb to above Nada Lake.*
- *The Bureau of Reclamation dammed Nada and Snow Lakes in the 1930s to supply the Leavenworth fish hatchery. A tunnel just under half a mile long releases water every summer (you can see the plume on the way down). The Bureau also built the trail back.*

*The descent:*

- *About 3,600 ft down along Snow Creek on switchbacks and straight trail, with two small talus fields.*
- *At Icicle Creek, make the sharp left into the parking lot.*

## *Leavenworth*

- *Stay in Leavenworth (2.5 hrs from Seattle, 3.25 from Spokane). Two small sporting goods stores in town, and dispersed camping in the area. [Town site](https://leavenworth.org).*
- *No dogs on the traverse (they disturb the native ptarmigan). There's a [pet lodge](https://www.leavenworthpetlodge.com) in town.*

## Shuttle

- Park at Snow Lakes Trailhead and take the [shuttle](https://www.leavenworthshuttle.com/mountain) to Stuart Lake Trailhead (about 20 minutes), finishing at your car. Book it after you have a permit and lodging.
- The lot holds about 50 cars. Arrive by 4:30am for the 5am shuttle to get a spot.
- Parking needs a Northwest Forest Pass, or an America the Beautiful pass. Overnight permits include two parking passes.
- For driving directions, use "Snow Lakes Trailhead" in Leavenworth, WA. "Snow Lake Trailhead" is about 2 hours away.
- *The Snow Lakes lot has a section for overnight permit holders. Overflow parks on the shoulder of Icicle Creek Road, following the signs.*
- *The shuttle waits at the Snow Lakes lot entrance. Arriving at 5:30am for a 5:45 shuttle has also worked.*
- *Stuart Lake Trailhead: the last stretch is potholed dirt, fine for low-clearance cars driven slowly. Overflow parks along the road in marked spots.*

## On the day

- **No-go**: skip it if rain is solidly forecast; wet granite slabs are unpleasant to descend. Thunderstorms have no real shelter in the Core Zone. Check smoke on [WatchDuty](https://app.watchduty.org).
- **Turnaround**: if you're far behind at Colchuck Lake, turn around. Plan B is out and back to Colchuck.
- **Water**: plenty of lakes, so carry about 1 L and filter everything. Refill at Tranquil Lake, the Lake Viviane outlet and Nada Lake (the last easy one).
- **Navigation**: many social trails. Follow cairns and the GPX; don't assume people ahead are on route.
- *Pace: the [trail guide's calculator](https://hikingguy.com/hikes/the-enchantments/#day-hike-pace-calculator) builds a timetable to each landmark. Starting in the dark is fine.*
- *Fuel: breakfast before the start, then eat at Colchuck, the top of Aasgard and Lake Viviane. Gels help on Aasgard.*
- *Terrain: rocks, roots and uneven ground; about 15% is extremely steep, sometimes with hands or butt sliding. The Lake Viviane to Snow Lakes descent scares some people afraid of heights, and a misstep there (around mile 10) could sprain an ankle.*
- *Early season: snow on Aasgard and in the Core Zone can hide weak snow bridges and avalanche zones. Water may mean scooping and melting snow.*
- *Sep and Oct can bring big temperature swings and early winter conditions.*
- *Altitude: not high, but Aasgard is near 8,000 ft, and sea-level hikers may feel short of breath.*
- *Mountain goats: give them space; they can get aggressive if they think you have salt. Pee on flat rocks off trail. If one gets aggressive, yell and stand your ground.*
- *Swimming: the lakes are cold, and a hiker drowned in Colchuck Lake in 2025. Skip it on a day hike.*
- *Smoke: also [Firesmoke](https://firesmoke.ca).*
- *Water: Snow Creek also has spots on the descent.*
- *The Chelan County Sheriff: if your fitness and preparation don't match the trail conditions, pick another trail.*
